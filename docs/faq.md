# Frequently asked questions

## Do I need to register a business before issuing invoices?

In most jurisdictions you can issue invoices as a sole trader or self-employed individual without forming a company, as long as you declare the income on your personal tax return. Whether you *should* incorporate depends on liability, tax efficiency and how much you bill annually. Speak to a local accountant before you cross a turnover threshold that triggers VAT, GST or similar registration.

## What is the difference between an invoice and a receipt?

An invoice is a *request* for payment; a receipt is a *confirmation* that payment has been received. The same transaction often produces both documents. Some clients ask for a paid invoice marked "PAID" with a date instead of a separate receipt; that is fine and is recommended for cash-flow record-keeping.

## Should I issue an invoice before or after the work is done?

For service work, the convention is to invoice *after* the work is delivered. For long projects, you can invoice in stages (deposit, milestones, final balance) so that you are not financing the project yourself. For one-off purchases of products, the invoice and the delivery usually accompany each other.

## Can I invoice in a currency other than my local one?

Yes. Many freelancers invoice in their client's currency to simplify the client's accounting. Always include the ISO currency code (USD, EUR, GBP) explicitly. Be aware that you will absorb the foreign-exchange spread when you receive payment, and that your accounting will record the amount in your local currency at the exchange rate on the date of the transaction (or the date of receipt; the rule varies by jurisdiction).

## Do I need to charge tax?

It depends on:

- Your country and whether you are registered for VAT, GST or sales tax.
- The location of your client.
- The type of service or product.

{doc}`jurisdictions` gives an overview for the US, EU and UK. The short answer for many freelancers is: if you are below the local registration threshold and selling services to business clients, you do not charge tax. Once you cross the threshold, registration becomes compulsory.

## How long should I keep invoice records?

Most jurisdictions require you to retain invoice records for between five and ten years for tax-audit purposes. Treat seven years as a conservative default unless your accountant tells you otherwise.

## What if I made a mistake on an invoice that has already been sent?

Do not silently edit the original. Issue a **credit note** that references the original invoice number and cancels (or partially cancels) the amount, then issue a new invoice with a new number for the correct amount. This keeps the audit trail clean and avoids accusations of altering records.

## Can I attach a contract or timesheet to the invoice?

Yes, and you often should. Attach supporting documents (timesheets, contracts, receipts for expenses being passed through) as separate PDFs, named in a way that makes them easy to file. Reference them in the body of the email so the client knows what to look for.

## Should I send an invoice in HTML email body or as a PDF?

PDF is strongly preferred. HTML emails get reflowed by email clients and look different on every system. A PDF is exactly what the client will print or archive, and most accounting systems can ingest a PDF attachment directly.

## What about electronic invoicing standards like PEPPOL?

If you sell to government or large enterprise customers in Europe, Australia or Singapore, they may require invoices delivered through an electronic invoicing network such as PEPPOL. This typically involves registering with an access point provider and emitting invoices as structured XML. For most small businesses this is overkill; for those that need it, the access point provider handles the format conversion from your normal invoice data.

## What is the simplest possible invoicing setup for a brand-new freelancer?

A spreadsheet template for the invoice itself, exported to PDF before sending; an online generator such as [invoicegenerator.run](https://invoicegenerator.run/) for the rare invoice that needs to look more polished; and a second spreadsheet (or a single tab in the same workbook) acting as your ledger. This setup will carry you cleanly through your first year. After that, the time you spend reconciling will start to outweigh the cost of a small-business accounting tool.
